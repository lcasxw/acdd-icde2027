A benchmark on an ordered stream can change which rows a decision rule may read, which trials are scored, and which unit counts as a failure. Sections II and III specify these choices using participant, session, trial and checkpoint keys, drawing on work on time-series benchmarking and event-time windows. In Section IV, replaying frozen EEG scores on a common suffix holds the evaluation rows fixed while the fitting budget changes. Section V measures how many participants lose more than two BA points at a fixed checkpoint and after averaging models. Section VI traces divergent bootstrap intervals to two registered inputs and quantifies how replacing a source calibrator changes participant counts. The contribution to data engineering is an empirical audit of access, aggregation and provenance in a cross-dataset benchmark.
